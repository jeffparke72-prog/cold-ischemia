Section I — The Problem

A System That Fails the People It Was Built to Serve

The living donor crisis is not a shortage of willing donors. It is a failure of intelligence — the failure to deploy AI where it is now demonstrably capable of preventing preventable attrition at every stage of the pipeline.

💀
103,000+
Patients on the National Kidney Waitlist
As of March 2024, 103,000+ Americans await a kidney transplant. Pretransplant mortality is 5.0 deaths per 100 patient-years (2023). Among adults newly listed in 2018–2020, a combined 26% had died or been removed due to deterioration within three years of listing.
OPTN, 2024 · Lentine et al., 2025 (SRTR Fig. KI 22)
🔬
90% vs 82%
5-Year Graft Survival: Living vs. Deceased Donor
Five-year graft survival is 90.0% for LDKT vs. 82.2% for deceased donor transplant (ages 18–34). A 2024 UK propensity-matched cohort of 10,915 transplants found LDKT associated with 6.03% lower five-year graft failure risk (95% CI 4.71–7.35%), representing 0.36 additional years of graft survival per recipient.
Lentine et al., 2025 · Buse et al., 2024 (British Journal of Surgery)
⚠️
15.2%
Candidates Withdraw — Fully Eligible — Before Any Decision
The LDCPR (2,107 consecutive candidates, 10 programs) confirmed 15.2% of non-approvals were due to candidate withdrawal before any formal decision. An additional 10.3% were denied for psychosocial reasons the literature characterizes as addressable support needs — not inherent medical barriers.
Lentine et al., 2021 (AJKD 78:3) — LDCPR
📊
255,000
DPMO — Current Pipeline Process Quality
Preventable attrition (withdrawal 15.2% + psychosocial 10.3%) = 25.5% of non-approvals = 255,000 Defects Per Million Opportunities. The current pipeline operates at ≈2.15σ — more than 3 standard deviations below the minimum acceptable healthcare standard of 3σ (66,807 DPMO).
CIF Analysis · Lentine et al., 2021
🌍
37%
Lower LDKT Likelihood for Black Recipients
A multicenter study of 19,287 recipients found Black recipients had 37% lower LDKT likelihood (aRR 0.63; 95% CI 0.59–0.67; p<0.001) independent of community vulnerability. Of all waitlisted Hispanic patients, only 5.2% received LDKT vs. 11.4% of non-Hispanic White patients. These disparities are structural, not clinical.
Axelrod et al., 2021 (JAMA Surgery) · Waterman et al., 2022
🧠
The Gap
Data Without Intelligence = Continued Inaction
OPTN collects program-level data. SRTR publishes it. USRDS tracks ESRD prevalence. The LDCPR documents failure modes. The problem is not absent data — it is the absence of computational intelligence to act on that data at the speed, scale, and specificity required to prevent the attrition it predicts. Project Donor-Flow closes that intelligence gap.

Section V — Awareness & Outreach

Awareness Is the Starting Line — Not the Finish Line

121K

AI-Profiled Campaign Reach

ODTA (2025) AI-driven donor profiling campaign reached 121,000 people and prompted 1,400 evaluation starts — a 1.16% activation rate far exceeding standard benchmarks. Three motivation archetypes (Caretakers, Purpose Seekers, Community-Motivated) drove the targeting.

80%

Of Awareness Investment Lost Without Pipeline

For every 100 people a campaign moves to inquiry, 80 will not complete the journey — not primarily from medical disqualification but from administrative burden, financial barriers, late psychosocial screening, and system neglect.

1,400

Evaluation Starts Require a Retention System

Without a high-retention evaluation system, the majority of those 1,400 ODTA-generated starters will be lost to the same structural failures the LDCPR documents. Project Donor-Flow is the infrastructure that converts starts into completions.

AI Doesn't Replace Outreach — It Aims It

Television advertising, billboards, faith community partnerships, employer outreach, HBCU engagement, and digital campaigns are the non-negotiable first act of any living donor strategy. Without them, the pipeline has no inputs. The ODTA campaign proves AI-targeted awareness produces measurably higher activation rates than broadcast approaches.

But the return on awareness investment is mathematically bounded by the retention capacity of the system that receives the donors it generates. A $500,000 awareness campaign without evaluation infrastructure improvement wastes $400,000 on donors the system will not retain. The marginal return on the first dollar of AI-enabled evaluation infrastructure investment increases the ROI of every awareness dollar already spent.

Stage 1 Predictive Donor Mapping identifies which ZIP codes, faith institutions, FQHCs, and community touchpoints have the highest concentration of individuals whose ML-derived profiles predict elevated donation intent among underrepresented populations. Stage 2 NLP monitoring converts the ambient social media conversation about kidney donation into a precision outreach instrument — identifying curious pre-inquiry donors before they self-identify to any transplant center.

Section VIII — Roadmap

From Pilot to National Standard in 36 Months

The programs that deploy Project Donor-Flow earliest are not simply adopting a better tool — they are building the evidence base that shapes what the national standard becomes.

Months 1–12  ·  Phase 1
Program-Level Pilot Deployment
2–3 transplant centers selected for geographic diversity, minimum 50 annual inquiries, and institutional commitment to Closed-Loop data sharing. All 8 AI stages deployed with monthly DMAIC Control metric review. Primary measure: donor conversion rate vs. historical baseline. Every interaction — completion and withdrawal — calibrates the DRS model. Data governance framework established for Phase 2.

Regional Scaling & Benchmark Establishment
Expansion to 10–15 programs, prioritizing the five critical states. National Donor Velocity Benchmark activates — inter-program comparison becomes meaningful accountability. Legislative coordination: CIF advocacy on anti-discrimination and reimbursement legislation synchronized with operational deployment in each priority state.

Section IX — Ethical Framework

AI That Serves the Donor, Not the System

Every Project Donor-Flow AI design choice reflects a foundational principle: the system serves the donor. Ethical requirements are not footnotes — they are architectural constraints.

Transparency & Explainability
Every AI output is explainable. The Donor Readiness Score shows donors exactly why their score is what it is and which support track they are being routed to. The Donor Passport is an AI-organized synthesis of documented data — not a black-box recommendation. No AI output produces a clinical decision without a human review layer. Model selection prioritizes interpretability at stages where clinical decisions depend on AI output (Nawar et al., 2026).
Data Privacy — Public Data Only
The Stage 2 NLP monitor classifies only publicly available social media posts — content users have voluntarily made public. No private communications, no non-public account data, no profiling of protected characteristics. This is functionally identical to public health departments analyzing public survey data to direct educational resources toward at-risk communities. AI makes the practice more precise; it does not change its legal or ethical character.
Equity Assurance by Architecture
The DRS model is trained with equity constraints that prevent race, educational attainment, and income from serving as direct inputs to routing decisions. The LDCPR's finding that Black race and lower education are independently associated with non-approval (Mader et al., 2024) means any model without equity correction would encode historical bias. Equity-adjusted outcome metrics are monitored quarterly; disparate results trigger immediate model audit and reweighting.
Donor Autonomy & Informed Consent
The AI-Concierge serves the donor, not the transplant program's conversion metrics. Velocity monitoring alerts the clinical team so they can address barriers — not apply pressure. Informed withdrawal is not a system failure; it is the system working correctly. Uninformed withdrawal — driven by avoidable administrative burden, financial anxiety, or an addressable support gap — is the failure the architecture is designed to prevent. The distinction is operationally significant and ethically essential.

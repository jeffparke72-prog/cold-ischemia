Tool 06 · Section VI · Survival Guide for Care Partners

The Moral Weight of Care

When caregiving stops being something you do and starts being something you are judged through — these tools help you find the line between what is required of you and what you have started to require of yourself.

Tool 01 of 05 · Moral saturation check
Where are you right now?

Moral saturation happens gradually. These eight questions are calibrated to the actual patterns that emerge in high-tension caregiving — not generic stress, but the specific weight of caregiving that has merged with identity. Answer honestly. There are no wrong answers here.

Tool 02 of 05 · The two-question separator
Separating what is required from what it means about you

When these two questions fuse, every action becomes a test of your worth. Keeping them separate is not a trick — it is a structural correction. Think of a specific recent situation that felt heavy and work through both questions about it.

The situation
Describe briefly what happened — or is happening — that brought you here.
Question one
What is actually required of me in this situation?
Not what you wish were required. Not the ideal version. The real, minimum functional requirement right now.
Question two
What am I telling myself this means about me?
The internal verdict — the thing you would never say aloud but that is running underneath every response.

Tool 04 of 05 · Compassion regulation
Compassion paused is not compassion lost

The lie that drives moral saturation is that compassion must be continuous to be real. It does not. When it becomes a condition you must maintain to keep the environment stable, it stops functioning as compassion.

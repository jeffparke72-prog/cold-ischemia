Cold Ischemia Foundation · December 24 – January 1

The Night Watch

An illustrated story you play as a care partner, across the eight nights from Christmas Eve to New Year's Day.

Holiday weeks are when the system thins out. Offices close, schedules shift, a doctor who has never met your person is on call, the pharmacy shelf comes up short, and somebody at the dinner table tells you you're making a fuss. Each night brings one real decision. Wrong turns teach rather than end the game, and you can replay any night. Your watch log fills as you go, and at the end you print a plan for your own holiday week.

About 10 minutes. Eight nights. Nothing you type leaves your device.

    Holiday Week Watch Plan

    December 24 – January 2 · kept for the person I care for

    Numbers to have before the 24th

    Emergency: 911. Suicide & Crisis Lifeline: call or text 988, any hour.

    My backup person

    • Has a copy of the one-page summary and this plan
    • Knows where the medications and the log are kept
    • Has agreed to take one real shift, so I can sleep

    The one-page summary (coat pocket)

    • Diagnoses, in plain words
    • Every medication, and allergies
    • Any line, port or device
    • Doctors and their numbers
    • Standing instructions ("fever means call")
    • Wishes, and who may speak for them

    Ask before the holiday schedule starts

    1. "What is the exact day and time of every appointment or treatment between December 24 and January 2, and can I have that in writing?"
    2. "Who is covering for our doctor that week, and how do we reach them after hours?"
    3. "Which symptoms mean call tonight, and which mean go to the emergency room?"
    4. "Will any prescription run short before normal pharmacy hours return? Can we fill it now?"
    5. "If there is a discharge on a holiday, who is the person we call afterward, and when is the follow-up?"

    Write down, every time

    DateTimeName & roleWhat was saidWhat was promisedNext step, by when

    If something goes wrong, climb in order: the person, their supervisor or charge nurse, a written grievance, then the outside agency. Keep a copy of every step.

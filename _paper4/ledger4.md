## THE NUMBER
- NONUSE (A) SRTR 2024 ADR: 29.3% of recovered deceased-donor kidneys not transplanted in 2024; 18.2% in 2013; 26.6% 2022; 27.9% 2023; biopsied kidneys 40.8% vs 6.4% not biopsied; donors 65+ 68.9% nonuse. Record 27,660 kidney transplants in 2024 (27,351 in 2023).
- REMOVALS (A) 2024: 3,743 candidates removed for death; 4,901 removed too sick (SRTR24K).
- HEALIO23 (B) 2022 OPTN data: 7,540 kidneys unused (26%); experts estimated 62% viable → 4,675 dialysis patients could have had a kidney. 
- ARITH (my estimate, flagged): 27,660 transplants − ~6,419 living donors (C, PhillyVoice) ≈ 21,200 deceased-donor transplants; /(1−0.293) ≈ 30,000 recovered; ≈ 8,800 unused; ≈ 24 per day. Present only as labelled arithmetic.
- OFFERS16 (A†/B) JAMA Netw Open 2019 (Husain, Mohan et al.): ~14 million offers 2008–2015 to >350,000 candidates; candidates who died received a median of 16 offers over 651 days; ~92.6–93% of declines attributed to organ/donor quality; ~76% of candidates had at least one viable offer; authors note some declines correct; kidneys declined many times don't have worse outcomes (prior research). NOTE: Healio 2023 phrase "each patient who dies had 16 kidneys rejected" overstates it: it is a MEDIAN.
- FRANCE (A) Aubert et al., JAMA Intern Med 2019;179(10):1365–74: 2004–2014 US recovered 156,089 deceased-donor kidneys, discarded 27,987 (17.9%); France 29,984 recovered, 2,732 discarded (9.1%); transplanted kidneys' mean donor age 36.5 (US) vs 50.9 (France); applying French model, 17,435 (62%) of US discards would have been transplanted; +132,445 allograft life-years over the decade (model estimate).
- INVESTIGATETV (B) 12 Feb 2026: one in five donated organs never transplanted; kidney waste ~25% (scope unclear); Georgia 17% of organs 2024.
- DISCARD-REASONS (B) NKF/others: "no recipient located" ~35% and "biopsy findings" ~29% leading reasons in one analysis; regulators' fear of outcome flags.
- BIOPSY (A) Procurement-biopsy reproducibility poor: single-institution: same category only 64% (kappa 0.25 overall, glomerulosclerosis 0.15); repeat biopsies kappa 0.12–0.17; only glomerulosclerosis >20% independently correlated with discard; 94% frozen sections.

## SCOREBOARD
- STAT16 (B) Casey Ross, STAT Aug 2016 (republished PBS NewsHour 13 Aug 2016): hospitals discarding organs/denying transplants to protect federal performance ratings; Bozorgzadeh co-authored study showing sharp rise in waitlist removals after 2007 standards; CMS changed benchmarks after; companion: 3,159 donated kidneys discarded in one year, up 20% from 2007; a 2016 CMS memo voiced concern programs avoid organs.
- SIA (A) Bowring et al., Am J Transplant 2018: 14 programs under CMS Systems Improvement Agreements vs 28 matched; 2006–2015 SRTR; after SIA kidney acceptance fell 5.9 percentage points (22%); SIA programs accepted 26.9% → 22.1% of offers; non-SIA 33.9% → 44.4%; largest drop for KDPI 0–40.
- OAR (A/B) SRTR offer acceptance ratio; OPTN MPSC began using it for review from summer 2023; flag <0.30 adults, <0.35 pediatric; offers counted only if accepted or declined before an accepted offer (discarded organs' offers excluded). IOTA efficiency domain (20 of 100 points) uses kidney offer acceptance ratio; no outcome data yet.
- MEMO25 (A†/B) OPTN/HRSA memorandum 6 Aug 2025 on allocation out of sequence (AOOS) warned of potential discipline; Husain et al. (NYU), policy period 6 Aug–23 Sep 2025: lower recovery, transplants, AOOS; if sustained ≈1,500 fewer transplants/yr; separate analysis summarised by SIDandT: kidney recovery fell from ~85 to ~76 per day, discard rates did not rise; AJT 2026 "There is nothing equitable about a wasted organ…" (30-day elimination of AOOS decreased utilization of hard-to-place kidneys). Unilateral AOOS rose 17-fold since 2020 (Healio Apr 2026).
- FILTERS (B) OPTN offer-filter pilot: centers avoided 67% of offers; recommended filters could bypass 59% of offers; some centers raised volume with up to 75% fewer offers; board approved opt-out default filters based on acceptance history (HRSA 2026).
- GANDER, ETC, MEDPAC: see v1. MedPAC Mar 2026: FFS dialysis Medicare margin 4.5% in 2024 (−0.2% 2023); 2026 projected 4%; 240,500 FFS beneficiaries, >7,600 facilities; industry letter disputes (≈2 points overstated).
- DUOPOLY (A†) Peer-reviewed study: DaVita+Fresenius share rose 59.1% → 77.1% (2005–2019); other estimates ~72%–80% (B/C).
- AKF-STEER (C) Allegations (2016 whistleblower) that two chains steered patients to AKF premium assistance; ~80% of AKF funding from the two chains (advocacy claim); California AB 290 enjoined 2019; AKF disputes. DOJ: 2014 DaVita corporate integrity agreement; July 2024 settlement >$34M alleged kickbacks (found only via a journal reference).
- VAC (B) DOJ complaint 2022 v. Fresenius Vascular Care: alleged 1,288 of 2,303 angioplasties among 60 patients unnecessary (ALLEGATION); American Access Care paid $3,594,791 (False Claims Act settlement, pre-2011 conduct). Industry: patients treated outside hospitals had 7.9% more fistulas, 9.8% fewer catheters (Healio Sept 2026 commentary, interested party).

## THE SHELF
- HMP-USE (A†) OPTN-based study (data through 2023): HMP used for 39% of deceased-donor kidneys; DGF 26% in 2023; 2021 circle allocation raised CIT and DGF.
- HMP-SURVEY (A) Concepcion et al., Clin Transplant 2026 (doi 10.1111/ctr.70637): 88 centers; centers using HMP = 35% of active centers but 63% of 2024 volume; HMP mostly run by OPOs (74% exclusively); most-cited barrier OPO refusal 58%; 92% endorse for selection, 90% for reducing DGF, 91% scheduling; 60% "sometimes" and 20% "yes" would decline a kidney if HMP could not be done.
- MOERS10 (A†/B) NEJM correspondence 5 Nov 2025 (doi 10.1056/NEJMc2406608): benefit persisted at 10 years; Groningen release: 79% vs 73% functioning; 99% follow-up response; vendor: 27% lower risk graft failure (ORS). Original NEJM 2009;360:7–19, 672 recipients.
- BELGIUM (A) National HMP reimbursement for ECD/DCD kidneys from Sept 2022; one-year outcomes and budget-impact analysis (PMC12657249).
- CONVINCE (A) NEJM 2023: 1,360 pts; all-cause mortality 17.3% HDF vs 21.9% HD; HR 0.77 (0.65–0.93); median 30 months; benefit mostly fewer infection deaths; CV mortality not significant; selection (convection ≥23 L). Fresenius 5008X FDA 510(k) Feb 2024 and updated May 2025; broader US launch 2026; ~160,000 US in-center machines eventually replaceable (company). 
- MYTEMP (A) Garg et al., Lancet 2022;400:1693–1703: cluster-randomised Ontario; personalised cooler dialysate vs 36.5 °C; composite CV death/hospitalisation 21.4% vs 22.4% not significant; no difference in intradialytic hypotension. (Chapter 4 of v3 omitted this: correct it.)
- HCV (A) Hopkins 2018 Annals (10 recipients), REHANNA, THINKER/EXPANDER; ~500 kidneys a year discarded for HCV-positive donors (2018 estimate); nonuse of HCV Ab+/NAT− kidneys 49.5% (2016) → 32.2% (2024) (SRTR).
- OXY (B/C) Foundation essays (../files): Baxter HemAssist (72% mortality increase per a secondary review), Hemosol (2003 trials stopped; insolvency mid-decade), Northfield PolyHeme (bankrupt 2009), Biopure (Oxyglobin dogs 1999; Hemopure South Africa 2001; FDA hold Apr 2003; rejected July 2003; SEC action; 2005 charges; Chapter 11 16 Jul 2009); 2008 pooled JAMA analysis; successor expanded access, 2022 IPO filing ~$30M. Oxyglobin approved for dogs (late 1990s). Labelled secondary: check originals.
- HEMO2LIFE (B/C) Hemarina, founder Franck Zal (Roscoff, CNRS/Sorbonne; spin-off 2007); M101 lugworm (Arenicola marina) extracellular hemoglobin; "40x more oxygen" company claim; OXYOP n=60, paired, 2016–2018; 4-year survival 98.3% vs 86% (company); CE mark; used in 2021 upper-limb transplant (India); Le Meur 2022 Artif Organs paper discloses conflict of interest; sources differ on trial size (60 vs 120).
- BHOC-CIF (C) CIF pages: BHOC Therapeutics/BHOC Transplant describe a research concept (cell-free bovine hemoglobin carrier for organ preservation/perfusion; kidney first); site itself states no outcome proof; "0 oxygen carriers with full FDA approval for IV use in humans".

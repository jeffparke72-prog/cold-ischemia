# The Hidden Rules
EPIGRAPH: "Tell me how you measure me, and I will tell you how I will behave."
BY: Eliyahu M. Goldratt, The Haystack Syndrome (1990)

Every system has two sets of rules. The first set is written down: the allocation policy, the conditions of participation, the payment schedule. The second set is the one people actually follow, and it is inferred from what happens to the people who break the first.

Part Two is about the second set. It begins with the oldest and best-documented of the hidden rules in this field: that a program is judged by the visible results of the kidneys it accepts and not by the invisible results of the kidneys it refuses. It follows that rule through a federal standard written in 2007, a corrective agreement that made programs less willing to say yes, and a metric introduced in 2023 that is meant to repair the damage and has, in my reading, a blind spot built into its center.

Then it turns to speed. A single memorandum, sent in August 2025, changed behavior across a national network within seven weeks. That is a remarkable fact about how this system listens, and it is not a fact that most people in the field have thought about. What does it mean that the system can change in seven weeks, and yet needed sixteen years to adopt a pump?

The part closes with two institutions that sit upstream and downstream of the kidney: the organ procurement organizations at the front door, whose lapses cost the public's trust in a matter of days, and the dialysis industry at the other end of the list, where the economics of the wait are written. I have tried to say, in each case, exactly what is established, what is alleged and what is unknown.

I would ask one thing of you in this part. When you read about a rule, a metric or a company, do not ask whether the people involved are good. Ask what the structure rewards. In my experience most disagreements in this field dissolve when you do.

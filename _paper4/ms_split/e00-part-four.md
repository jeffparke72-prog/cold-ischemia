# From Anger to Plan
EPIGRAPH: "As our case is new, so we must think anew, and act anew."
BY: Abraham Lincoln, Annual Message to Congress (1862)

Most books of this kind end with an appeal to awareness. I have little patience for it, and the reason is that the people who will read this book are, for the most part, already aware. They have waited, or watched someone wait. What they lack is not information about the problem but a short list of specific, answerable demands, each addressed to a named institution, with a number attached and a date beside it.

Part Four is that list. Chapter 13 sets out fifteen moves, grouped by the institution that must make them. For each there is a mechanism, the evidence from the earlier chapters that supports it, a risk, and a test you can check in a year. Chapter 14 turns the list into a ninety-day plan for a patient, a care partner, a clinic or a community group, with twelve questions to ask, three letters to send, and a one-page log to keep. Chapter 15 steps back one more time and asks what we owe a gift.

A word about anger. It is a legitimate response to a kidney that never reached a body and a person who never left the chair. But anger without a plan is a feeling. With a plan, it is fuel.

Why I put CIF behind this

I didn't come to BHOC as an investor or a scientist. I came to it the way I come to everything in this work — as someone who has spent nearly two decades on the losing end of the clock every donor organ is racing against. Cold ischemia time isn't an abstraction to me; it's the number that, more than almost anything else in the transplant process, quietly decides how long a graft is going to last. For years, the industry's answer to it has been a cooler and a stopwatch.

What makes BHOC's lineage matter to me isn't a marketing claim — it's that the class of molecule it descends from has real, published, peer-reviewed evidence of doing something ice alone cannot: actively fighting ischemia-reperfusion injury during the exact window that predicts delayed graft function, and delayed graft function is one of the strongest known predictors of how long a transplanted kidney survives. That's innovation aimed at the right target — not faster logistics, but the biology of longevity itself.

I'm not going to overstate where BHOC specifically stands. You'll see the honest gap in its own evidence laid out plainly below, because a foundation that hides its own gaps has no business asking federal agencies to close theirs. But I find the underlying science compelling enough, and the stakes for patients like me high enough, that closing that gap is worth this Foundation's voice. Better perfusion technique isn't a footnote in transplant medicine — it may be the most underfunded lever we have for graft survival.

— Jeff Parke, Founder, Cold Ischemia Foundation

Executive Summary

What this page is, and isn't.

A plain-language, citation-backed briefing on the science of oxygen-carrying perfusion additives in kidney transplantation — for advocates, patients, and caregivers who want the real evidence, not the pitch deck.

Well established

Peer-reviewed, published, reproducible

Adding an oxygen carrier — red cells, perfluorocarbon particles, or a hemoglobin-based molecule like HBOC-201 or M101 — to a kidney's perfusion or preservation solution measurably reduces markers of ischemic injury (lactate, pro-inflammatory cytokines, adhesion molecules) versus no carrier at all, in animal models and a small number of human studies. The largest human cohort (M101, n=58) showed fewer recipients needing repeat dialysis and faster return to normal kidney function.

Still open

Investigational, early-stage, or unproven

No hemoglobin-based oxygen carrier has full U.S. regulatory approval for any human IV use, and none is approved specifically as a kidney-perfusion adjunct. Long-term graft survival data (5-year, 10-year) for carrier-perfused kidneys doesn't yet exist in the literature. BHOC's own publicly stated applications are in trauma and surgical blood loss — see BHOC Today for the honest state of that record.

Every minute a donor kidney sits without its native blood supply, its cells lose oxygen and begin a cascade of injury that peaks not during storage, but at the moment blood flow is restored. An oxygen carrier's entire premise is interrupting that cascade before reperfusion ever happens.

Why Oxygen Delivery Is the Bottleneck

Cold ischemia & ischemia-reperfusion injury.

Two related but distinct injuries threaten every transplanted kidney before it meets its new blood supply — and they set the ceiling on how well that graft will function, for how long.

The clock

Cold ischemia time (CIT)

Standard cold storage slows metabolism, not stops it — cells still consume dissolved oxygen and produce waste. Hypoxia depletes ATP, disables the sodium-potassium pump, and lets calcium and sodium flood into cells. In the largest published organ-preservation study of this class, mean CIT was 740 minutes — over 12 hours.

The paradox

Ischemia-reperfusion injury (IRI)

The worst damage often happens in the seconds after blood flow is restored, not during storage. Reintroduced oxygen reacts with accumulated metabolic byproducts to generate reactive oxygen species, triggering an inflammatory cascade (IL-6, IL-8, TNF-α) linked to acute rejection risk.

Why this predicts long-term graft survival, not just day-one function: Delayed graft function (DGF) — the clinical marker of severe IRI — is consistently linked in registry studies to higher rates of acute rejection and reduced graft half-life. Anything that measurably reduces IRI markers during the ischemic window is, mechanistically, working on the same pathway that determines whether a kidney is still functioning in five or ten years — even though, as the Evidence tab makes clear, nobody has yet published that long-term follow-up specifically for carrier-perfused kidneys.

127 Years of Trying to Bottle Oxygen Delivery

A history of hemoglobin-based oxygen carriers.

The idea is over a century old. The record includes real breakthroughs, a federally scrutinized safety controversy, and a field that only recently pivoted from "blood substitute for trauma" toward "organ preservation adjunct."

1898

First recorded attempt

Von Stark administers subcutaneous hemoglobin to anemic patients; the formulation cannot be stabilized — the core problem the field will spend a century solving is already visible.

1916

Sellards & Minot

33 subjects receive infusions of stroma-free hemoglobin; most tolerate it acutely, but all develop hemoglobinuria — an early signal of kidney-specific toxicity.

1937

The Amberson case

A postpartum hemorrhage patient given stroma-free hemoglobin when cross-matched blood is unavailable develops oliguria and dies of renal failure.

1962

Structural breakthrough

Perutz and Kendrew's Nobel Prize-winning 3D hemoglobin structure work makes rational molecular modification possible for the first time.

1990s

Generation 2 — cross-linked hemoglobin

Baxter's HemAssist (DCLHb) chemically cross-links the hemoglobin tetramer, extending circulatory half-life from ~2 hours to 6–12 hours.

1999

HemAssist trial halted

A JAMA trauma trial (n=112) reports 28-day mortality of 46% in the DCLHb group vs. 17% on saline (P=0.003). Baxter discontinues the program.

2001

Hemopure (HBOC-201) approved — South Africa

Biopure's bovine-derived polymerized hemoglobin becomes the first HBOC approved anywhere for routine human use, followed by Russia. It has never received full FDA approval; U.S. access remains limited to expanded-access and compassionate-use programs.

1998

Oxyglobin approved — veterinary use

Biopure's companion HBOC is approved for dogs — to date the field's clearest regulatory success, and still limited to animals.

2008

The Natanson meta-analysis

A JAMA systematic review of 16 trials across five HBOC products finds a pooled, statistically significant increase in myocardial infarction and death. The finding still shapes FDA posture toward the entire drug class today.

2009

PolyHeme ends the program

Northfield Laboratories' phase III trial (n=714) shows 30-day mortality of 13% vs. 10% for controls, with higher serious-adverse-event rates. The product is discontinued.

2013–14

MP4/Hemospan halted

A non-significant mortality trend (57% vs. 50%, P=0.18) in a phase II trauma trial; development stops for funding and regulatory reasons before the question is ever answered definitively.

2019

The field pivots: organ preservation

Published research begins testing HBOC-201 specifically as a red-cell substitute in ex vivo kidney machine perfusion — a sealed, monitored circuit rather than open circulation.

2020

M101 first-in-human organ study

Le Meur et al. publish the first human safety and proof-of-principle data for M101 (marine lugworm hemoglobin, HEMO2life) in kidney preservation — the largest published human HBOC-in-transplant cohort to date (n=58).

2022–24

Comparative & mechanistic studies

Multicenter cohort comparisons and ex vivo immunogenicity studies build the mechanistic case for why oxygen carriers reduce inflammatory injury markers during perfusion.

Present

Next-generation candidates

Encapsulated, recombinant, and hybrid-design HBOCs enter early trials — alongside commercially positioned successor molecules such as BHOC, which trace their lineage to Hemopure. See BHOC Today below.

Cell & Circuit Level

How an oxygen carrier protects a kidney in perfusion.

Three families of oxygen carrier have been tested in kidney perfusion circuits. They deliver oxygen by different physical mechanisms, but the downstream biology they're fighting is the same.

The injury cascade an oxygen carrier interrupts

Hypoxia

ATP depletion

Without dissolved O₂, oxidative phosphorylation stops; ion pumps fail; water floods the cell.

Accumulation

Metabolite build-up

Anaerobic metabolism raises lactate and hypoxanthine — substrate for the oxidative burst to come.

Reperfusion

ROS burst

Reintroduced O₂ reacts with accumulated substrate, damaging endothelium.

Inflammation

Cytokine cascade

IL-6, IL-8, TNF-α and ICAM-1 rise, recruiting immune cells — the axis linked to rejection risk.

In a controlled porcine normothermic perfusion study: IL-6 fell from 1.04 ± 0.27 ng/mL (control) to 0.11 ± 0.11 ng/mL with M101 (p<0.0001). 4-hour lactate accumulation fell from 6,318 ± 681 µM (control) to 4,290 ± 1,625 µM with M101 (p<0.05) and 3,843 ± 1,091 µM with a PFC carrier (p<0.01). Histology showed no additional injury attributable to any carrier.

What's Actually Been Published

Clinical & pre-clinical evidence, by study.

Ranked from largest human cohort to earliest mechanistic work. Every number below is drawn directly from the cited paper — see References for full citations.

Human · n=58

M101 (HEMO2life) — first-in-human

Le Meur et al., American Journal of Transplantation, 2020

58 kidney recipients from 60 deceased donors (38% extended-criteria), mean CIT 740 minutes, M101 added at 1 g/L. Dialysis-requiring DGF: 6.9% vs. 26.1% in a comparison cohort (P=.038). Time to normal creatinine: 7 vs. 13 days (P=.020). 490 adverse events (41 serious) in 3 months; independent safety board found no causal link between M101 and two thrombosis-related graft losses; no immunologic, allergic, or prothrombotic signal detected.

Human cohort comparison

HEMO2life vs. multicenter registry

Le Meur et al., Artificial Organs, 2022

Follow-up comparison against a large multicenter prospective cohort found HEMO2life-treated kidneys showed improved renal function outcomes independent of cold ischemia time — suggesting the benefit is an active protective effect, not just a proxy for shorter storage.

Porcine · ex vivo

Oxygen carrier immunogenicity

Frontiers in Transplantation, 2023–24

4-hour normothermic perfusion (n=3/group) comparing RBCs, PFC particles, and M101 against no-carrier controls. All carriers reduced IL-6, IL-8, TNF-α, and ICAM-1 vs. control; lactate accumulation was significantly lower with both PFC and M101. Histology showed no carrier-attributable harm.

Porcine · pre-clinical

HBOC-201 vs. blood, subnormothermic perfusion

Transplantation (LWW), 2019

Compared HBOC-201 to whole blood as the oxygen source during ex vivo subnormothermic machine perfusion, evaluating renal protection against ischemia-reperfusion injury — part of the pre-clinical case that acellular carriers can substitute for blood in a closed perfusion circuit.

Human · safety

M101 large-animal & primate safety

Int'l Journal of Molecular Sciences, 2025

Evaluated intravenous M101 safety in dogs and monkeys — part of the ongoing toxicology package supporting expanded human use beyond the original first-in-human organ-preservation study.

Class-wide · meta-analysis

The systemic safety signal

Natanson et al., JAMA, 2008

Pooled 16 trials across five HBOC products used as IV blood substitutes (not organ perfusion) and found a statistically significant increase in myocardial infarction and death — the single biggest reason no HBOC has cleared full FDA approval, and essential context for every organ-preservation study on this page.

The honest read on graft survival, specifically: every published human study of HBOCs in kidney transplantation measures short-term renal recovery — DGF, time to normal creatinine, early biopsy findings. None yet reports 1-, 5-, or 10-year graft survival for carrier-perfused kidneys. The mechanistic story is strong and biologically coherent; the direct long-term outcome data doesn't exist in the literature yet. That gap is worth naming plainly.

The Record the Field Has to Reckon With

Why no HBOC has full FDA approval.

Understanding the 2008 controversy is essential to evaluating any claim about this molecule class honestly — BHOC included.

The finding

Natanson et al., JAMA 2008

A pooled analysis of 16 randomized trials testing five different HBOC products as IV blood substitutes found a statistically significant increase in both myocardial infarction and all-cause mortality versus controls.

The proposed mechanism

Nitric oxide scavenging

Free hemoglobin outside a red cell scavenges nitric oxide, a key vasodilator, causing systemic and coronary vasoconstriction. A 2025 re-analysis (Estep) suggested autoxidation and oxidative stress may matter more than vasoconstriction alone.

Why organ perfusion is a different exposure profile

Systemic IV use (the 2008 finding)Ex vivo organ perfusion (this page's focus)
CirculationPatient's entire cardiovascular systemClosed circuit through one isolated organ
DurationHours to days, systemicTypically hours, flushed before implantation
NO-scavenging consequenceSystemic hypertension, coronary vasoconstrictionLocalized to a non-beating, often cross-clamped organ
Regulatory statusNo full approval anywhere for IV trauma/surgical useNo dedicated approval yet; studied under research protocols

This distinction is why organ-preservation researchers kept working with these molecules even after 2008 — but it's a scientific argument, not a regulatory one. No agency has issued a specific safety finding clearing HBOCs for perfusion use; the studies above are the current best evidence, not a settled approval.

Separating the Brand From the Bench

Where BHOC actually stands right now.

BHOC is positioned publicly as a next-generation, bovine-derived successor to Hemopure (HBOC-201). Here is exactly what is and isn't documented about it, stated plainly.

What BHOC's own materials claim

Positioning & design

Described as combining clinical experience from Hemopure with newer development approaches. Particle size is cited as roughly 1,000× smaller than a red blood cell, said to reach microvasculature RBCs cannot, and to deliver oxygen without dependence on 2,3-DPG. Stated target applications are orthopedic and critical surgery, blood-shortage or infection-risk situations, transfusion-restricted patients, rare blood types, and cardiac or microcirculation-compromised care.

What's not yet documented

Kidney perfusion & transplantation

Publicly available BHOC materials do not currently mention kidney perfusion, organ preservation, cold ischemia, or transplantation among its stated applications or evidence base. No peer-reviewed, published clinical or pre-clinical trial of BHOC specifically in a kidney-perfusion circuit was located in the literature search behind this page. That doesn't mean the science is wrong — the underlying mechanism is the same class studied in the M101 and HBOC-201 trials above — but a claim that "BHOC protects graft survival in kidney transplantation" is currently an extrapolation from the class-wide evidence, not a finding from a BHOC-specific study.

The strongest version of this story is also the honest one: HBOC-class molecules have real, published, statistically significant evidence of reducing ischemia-reperfusion injury and improving short-term kidney recovery after transplant. BHOC inherits that class-wide scientific rationale and a stated design lineage from Hemopure. What it doesn't yet have is its own kidney-transplant trial data — and advocacy that keeps that line clear is both more defensible and more persuasive to a clinical or regulatory audience.

What would close the gap

Step 1

Ex vivo pre-clinical study

Porcine or human-discard kidney perfusion comparing BHOC to M101/HBOC-201 on the same IRI biomarker panel used above.

Step 2

Toxicology package

Species-appropriate safety data specific to the perfusion-circuit exposure profile, not just systemic IV use.

Step 3

First-in-human proof-of-principle

A small cohort study modeled on the 2020 M101 design — safety plus early renal-function signal.

Step 4

Long-term graft follow-up

The gap no HBOC has closed yet, BHOC included — 1-, 5-, 10-year graft survival data.

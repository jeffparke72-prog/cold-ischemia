Living Donor Hub · free · no account

Every living donor deserves a clear path, and every patient a fair chance to be offered one.

Find transplant programs near you, write a message that actually gets answered, put a private packet together, and read the research behind the decision. Built on the foundation’s independence: no one here is paid to steer you.

Step one

Who is this for?

Pick the one that fits today. You can change it any time, and everything stays on your own device unless you choose to send it.

Our independence, plainly: the foundation takes no money from pharmaceutical companies, dialysis companies or hospital systems, and nothing on this page is an advertisement. We also don’t ask you for money. This is educational and is not medical or legal advice; your transplant team makes every clinical decision.
Step two

Find programs near you

These are U.S. transplant programs from the federal government’s own facility data, grouped by the organ procurement organization (OPO) that serves the area. Add up to five to your shortlist, then call and confirm they run a living donor program.

The data lists which programs perform each type of transplant. It doesn’t say which ones run a living donor program, so always ask. CMS tier 1 is the top performance tier for the local OPO, and tier 3 is the lowest. The OPO phone number is from federal records and the OPO can point you to the right place. Prefer a second opinion? Compare programs’ outcomes at srtr.org and see every OPO in the U.S. OPO Directory.
Step three

Say it well, once

Choose a few things and the message writes itself. Edit it freely. Nothing is sent from this page; you decide where it goes.

Step four

Read what the evidence actually says

A curated shelf of official guides, peer-reviewed studies, law and reporting. Every link goes to the original source. Star the ones you want to bring to your appointment and they’ll be added to your packet.

Links were gathered from public search results and point to each publisher’s own page. Publishers sometimes move pages, so if one doesn’t open, search its title. Summaries are ours and are not a substitute for reading the source.

Step five

Your packet, in your hands

Put your shortlist, your message and your reading list in one place. Take it to a transplant program yourself, or, only if you choose, send a copy to the foundation.

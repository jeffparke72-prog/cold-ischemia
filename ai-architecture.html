Project Donor-Flow — Research Framework

The eight-stage AI architecture proposed to close the living donor pipeline.

From "The Intelligence Gap: How Artificial Intelligence Can End America's Living Donor Crisis" — Cold Ischemia Foundation's peer-reviewed white paper. Click each stage to see what it proposes and the published research behind it.

This is a proposed research framework, not deployed software. No live AI system runs on this page or anywhere on this site. Each stage below is grounded in real, cited, published research on AI applications in living donation — but Project Donor-Flow as an integrated system has not been built or clinically validated.

This framework addresses donor identification, screening, and retention — not prediction of long-term graft function or outcome-optimized donor-recipient matching. Those are a different research question this document does not claim to answer.

The Pipeline

Eight stages, one continuous system.

Each stage below deploys a specific, cited AI method. Click to expand.

1

Population Identification

Random Forest ML Model
▼

Trained on ESRD prevalence by ZIP code (USRDS), CDC Social Vulnerability Index scores, and OPTN regional living donor rates, this model identifies where potential donors and unmet need are geographically concentrated — so outreach resources go where they'll matter most, instead of being spread evenly regardless of local burden.

Cites: Boadu et al., 2023, Frontiers in Public Health

2

Social Media Intelligence

Natural Language Processing
▼

Continuously analyzes public social media discussion of living donation to identify people expressing curiosity or stated barriers, before they ever contact a transplant center — routing each person toward content addressing their specific concern rather than generic awareness messaging.

Cites: Nawar et al., 2026, Frontiers in Transplantation; ODTA, 2025

3

Intelligent Intake & the Donor Readiness Score

Natural Language Understanding
▼

Processes a donor's first communication in real time, assessing intent, emotional state, and logistical constraints, and assigns a Donor Readiness Score (DRS) — not to screen someone out, but to route the level of support they need to stay engaged. A low score triggers more support, not rejection.

Cites: Al Awadhi et al., 2025, Clinical Transplantation (Houston Methodist)

4

Automated Clinical Pre-Screen

FHIR API Integration
▼

Pulls medical records automatically under the 21st Century Cures Act's FHIR interoperability mandate, eliminating the burden of a donor manually tracking down their own records — a burden the paper identifies as falling disproportionately on donors with lower health literacy.

Cites: Bossini et al., 2023 (LSS/DMAIC applied to donor evaluation)

5

Adaptive Psychosocial Assessment

AI-Administered, Clinician-Enhanced
▼

Deploys the Altruism Assessment Scale and Coercion Risk Inventory as adaptive digital instruments before the first in-person visit — aiming to reduce the social-desirability bias that makes donors present as more freely motivated in a face-to-face evaluation than they may actually feel.

Cites: Lentine et al., 2021 (LDCPR data on non-approval factors)

6

The Concierge Support Engine

Velocity Monitoring
▼

Tracks whether a donor's progress is falling outside the expected time window for their stage, and triggers proactive outreach before the donor disengages entirely — the paper's answer to the well-documented pattern of donors quietly dropping out of a slow process.

Cites: ODTA, 2025 (Houston Methodist human-technology co-care model)

7

AI-Standardized Multi-Disciplinary Review

Decision-Support Dashboard
▼

The final approval decision stays a human clinical judgment — but the dashboard flags when evaluation criteria vary between programs for the same donor characteristic, surfacing inconsistency rather than deciding for the team.

Framework component; see Section 3.7 of the source white paper

8

Post-Donation AI Monitoring

Closed-Loop Learning
▼

Extends OPTN's required 6/12/24-month follow-up with continuous monitoring, and feeds every outcome — positive and negative — back into the DRS model as a training data point, so the scoring improves over time instead of staying static.

Framework component; see Section 3.8 of the source white paper

The Scoring Model

The Donor Readiness Score, as defined in the paper.

DRS Formula
DRS = w₁(Clinical Pre-Screen) + w₂(Psychosocial Signal) + w₃(Financial Status) + w₄(Logistical Capacity)

Where the weights (w₁–w₄) sum to 1.0 and are calibrated quarterly against LDCPR outcome data. The design principle: the score doesn't ask "is this donor qualified?" — it asks what a specific donor needs right now to stay engaged in the process.

≥70
High DRS — Fast-Track
40–69
Moderate — Standard + Support
<40
Low — Support Track Priority

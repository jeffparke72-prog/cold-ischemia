# A Note on Sources and Honesty
EPIGRAPH: "A false balance is abomination to the Lord: but a just weight is his delight."
BY: Proverbs 11:1 (King James Version, 1611)

A book that asks for action owes its readers an account of how far its facts can be trusted. I owe you that, and I would rather over-explain than leave you with a false confidence.

**What this book is.** It is an advocacy narrative written by a patient-community founder, and not a systematic review. It draws on published studies, registry reports, news accounts, government notices and the history of the field. It does not present new research of its own.

**How I checked.** The searches for this book were run in October 2026 using summaries of published material. In many cases I could read only a summary and not the full paper. Where that is so, the Source Notes in Appendix A carry a grade. Grade A means a primary document or registry whose key figures I confirmed. Grade B means a credible secondary account. Grade C means a single source, an advocacy statement or a manufacturer's claim. A dagger (†) marks bibliographic details, such as authors or page numbers, that are only partly confirmed. A reader preparing this for publication should complete those from the original papers.

**History is older than six months.** The early chapters describe events from 1943 to 1972. They are history, and I have used the retellings that appear most consistently in published accounts. I have hedged details that vary between sources and have flagged in the Source Notes that the primary texts should be consulted before any future edition.

**Composites.** Where a person appears without a full name or with a first name only, Marisol, Daniel, Aisha, Walt, Ray, Andre, and the unnamed men and women in hallways and clinics, the person is invented to stand for many. They are marked as such in the text. The named people in this book, among them Kolff, Scribner, Quinton, Shields, Murray, the Herrick brothers, Glazer, Slayman, Pisano, Looney and Andrews, are named because their stories are part of the public record, and I have limited what I say about them to what that record supports. I have not invented their words or their feelings.

**Where the evidence runs out.** I have tried to say so each time. Some figures are old, such as the emergency-only dialysis studies from 2018. Some come from small studies, such as the mentoring trials and the intensive-dialysis pregnancy cohorts. Some come from manufacturers or advocacy groups. A claim in this book is only as strong as its Source Note.

**An interest to declare.** The author founded the Cold Ischemia Foundation, which publishes information on kidney preservation technologies, including some mentioned in Chapter 10. The Foundation accepts no donations, grants or sponsorships. I mention the connection so that you can weigh that chapter accordingly.

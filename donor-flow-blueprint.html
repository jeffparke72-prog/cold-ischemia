Section II · Diagnostic Analysis
The Anatomy of the “Leaky Bucket”
🔍
Human-Centric Triage Failure

Overwhelmed coordinators default to subconscious triage bias. LDCPR data confirms candidates most likely to be approved were married and college-educated; those least likely included Black race, elevated blood pressure, and smoking history. This is not a coordinator failure — it is the structural consequence of high-volume decision-making with no data-driven decision support.

Root Cause: Absent Decision-Support Infrastructure
🚫
The Psychosocial Vacuum

Psychosocial screening occurs reactively and late. Published sub-analysis confirms 10.3% of non-approvals are psychosocial: poor adherence (62%), psychiatric diagnosis (50%), cognitive concerns (33%). Zero standardized early-intervention protocols exist nationally to convert these from disqualifiers into support targets. The system discards rather than rehabilitates.

Root Cause: Late-Stage Reactive Protocol / No Remediation Path
⚙️
Logistical Friction & Evaluation Fatigue

The LDCPR confirms candidate withdrawal accounts for 15.2% of all single-reason non-approvals — donors who were medically and psychosocially eligible but exited due to administrative burden alone. These are not medical disqualifications. They are entirely preventable operational failures. The current system has no mechanism to address what its own data predicts.

Root Cause: Passive Donor Architecture — 15.2% preventable loss
📦
Data Fragmentation & State-Level Siloing

OPTN state data reveals dramatic geographic disparity in living donor rates with no clinical explanation — only a systemic one. Mississippi (40.8% obesity, AKF grade F), Louisiana, and Alabama show critical decline. These outcomes are predictable from policy data, which makes them preventable. Continued inaction in the face of preventable outcomes is a choice, not a constraint.

Root Cause: No National Interoperability or Equity Correction Standard

Why Living Donor Candidates
Are Denied — The Verified Data

Data from the Living Donor Collective Pilot Registry (10 programs, 2,107 consecutive U.S. candidates) establishes the structural distribution of denial reasons. Approximately 50% of all evaluated living kidney donor candidates nationally are denied before surgery. Understanding which categories are remediable and building protocols to target them is the clinical foundation of this framework.

Primary Denial Reason Distribution — 594 Single-Reason Non-Approvals

Source: Lentine et al. 2021, AJKD 78(3):394–405. 10 U.S. programs, 2,107 consecutive candidates through March 2020.

~50%
Denied Before Surgery — National Rate

Approximately half of all evaluated living kidney donor candidates nationally do not reach surgery. The 2025 ScienceDirect study (813 evaluated, 194 denied = 24% at one center) and LDCPR multi-center data confirm this magnitude. Attrition including pre-evaluation withdrawal is substantially higher.

15.2%
Withdrawal — 100% Preventable

Candidate withdrawal accounts for 15.2% of all single-reason non-approvals. These donors were medically and psychosocially eligible. They were not rejected — they were lost to process friction. Every withdrawal is a system failure. The AI-Concierge Velocity Monitoring system targets this category specifically.

10.3%
Psychosocial — Largely Remediable

Poor adherence (62%), psychiatric diagnosis (50%), cognitive concerns (33%), poor social support (25%). All benefit meaningfully from early identification and targeted support. These are support needs the current system treats as exit conditions. This framework recategorizes them as early-intervention triggers with defined protocols.

0.5%
Economic — Radically Undercounted

Formally recorded in only 0.5% of single-reason denials — dramatically understating the true barrier. Economic pressure suppresses initial inquiry and causes withdrawal classified elsewhere. The Financial Advocacy Track targets this at first contact, before it operates invisibly upstream of formal evaluation.

47.9%
Medical (All Types)

Largest category. Hypertension is most common at 9.8%. Diabetes, obesity (BMI thresholds vary ≥30 to ≥35 by center), and cardiac risk follow. Threshold variability creates inequitable access based on which center a donor contacts, not clinical evidence.

18.5%
CKD Risk Factors

Family history, elevated creatinine, proteinuria, reduced GFR — relative contraindications with wide center variability. Modifiable factors (weight, blood pressure) directly targeted by the Metabolic Support Protocol before they reach formal evaluation thresholds.

13.6%
Recipient Reason

Recipient became ineligible or received an organ through another channel during the donor’s evaluation. This is pipeline velocity failure. AI-accelerated intake reduces this by shortening total evaluation duration before recipient status changes.

10.3%
Psychosocial

Poor adherence (62%), psychiatric diagnosis (50%), cognitive concerns (33%), poor social support (25%). Early AI-administered adaptive screening converts a substantial proportion from denial to approval through support protocols initiated at identification, not at formal screening.

Core Technology Architecture

Donor Readiness Score (DRS)

A dynamic, real-time triage score incorporating clinical, psychosocial, financial, and logistical variables — assigned at first contact and updated continuously. Replaces subjective coordinator judgment with reproducible, evidence-based triage applying identical criteria to every donor. High-DRS routes to fast-track. Low-DRS routes to targeted support. No donor exits without a documented protocol response.

🔗
FHIR API Clinical Integration

Automated medical record retrieval through the FHIR standard already mandated by the 21st Century Cures Act. Medical history pre-populated before the first in-person visit. Directly targets the 15.2% candidate withdrawal rate driven by administrative burden. Regulatory alignment — not technology speculation.

💬
Natural Language Understanding

AI initiates conversations — not forms. High-dimensional NLP processes multi-channel inquiries in real time, assessing intent, financial status, and logistical constraints on first contact. Sentiment analysis detects coercion, financial anxiety, and motivation decline through contextual comprehension. The difference between this and a static form is the difference between retaining 85% of inquiries and losing most to friction.

A real-time dashboard delivered to every team member before each donor interaction. AAS and CRI scores, logistical stall points, financial concern markers, and full communication history — unified and current. Conversations begin: “I see you’ve been held up on the financial forms since Tuesday — I have a solution.” That contextual specificity is what retains donors close to withdrawal.

AAS and CRI administered as adaptive digital instruments — questions reshape in real time based on prior responses. AI pre-administration reduces social desirability bias documented in face-to-face screening. The Full Cost of Inaction — Measured in Dialysis Years

Every lost donor inquiry is a compounding fiscal burden. Dialysis averages approximately $90,000 per patient per year (USRDS 2023). Every living donor transplant the current system fails to produce represents years of that cost absorbed by Medicare, Medicaid, and private insurers — plus preventable mortality and reduced quality of life. The healthcare system has been absorbing this cost invisibly for decades. Project Donor-Flow makes it visible and directly addressable through operational improvement.

02
Coordinator Reallocation — Efficiency Through Elevation

The AI-Concierge transfers 60% of coordinator administrative burden to the system without eliminating staff. Clinical capacity is reallocated to high-value psychosocial counseling that only skilled humans can perform. The same headcount produces dramatically more transplants because it is concentrated on the work that matters. This is efficiency through elevation — not attrition — and it is the mechanism that makes national scaling economically viable without proportional capital investment.

03
Standardization as Institutional Liability Architecture

The LDCPR confirms denial criteria vary significantly across programs — candidates with identical profiles receiving materially different outcomes at different centers. That variability is a documented legal exposure without a discoverable audit trail. AI-orchestrated standardization eliminates the variability while producing auditable, defensible protocol documentation for every evaluation decision. This argument resonates equally with clinical leadership and institutional legal counsel.

04
Marginal Cost Growth Against Exponential Clinical Return

A 40% conversion rate increase does not require a 40% infrastructure investment. The AI-Concierge handles volume growth through existing clinical infrastructure at marginal cost. Early adopters build the evidence base that shapes the national standard — a reputational, competitive, and regulatory positioning advantage in a field increasingly driven by measurable outcomes data. The Cold Ischemia Foundation operates with zero pharmaceutical, dialysis, or insurance funding — by founding constraint. Every recommendation is made without commercial interest.

For Psychosocial Team Leads
What This Framework Does for Your Clinical Practice
  • The Donor Passport is built for your expertise — not as a replacement for it. When you connect with a donor, you arrive with AAS scores, CRI results, logistical stall history, financial concern markers, and the complete communication timeline already synthesized. You spend the conversation addressing the donor’s actual situation — not the first 20 minutes discovering it from scratch.
  • Predictive Psychosocial Care means you intervene at the moment of maximum clinical influence — before the donor has hardened a withdrawal decision. The AI detects behavioral precursors of withdrawal and alerts you before it occurs. The difference between a proactive 10-minute call and a post-withdrawal exit note is the operational difference between retaining a donor and documenting a preventable loss.
  • The Closed-Loop Refinement system creates what does not exist in this field today: a systematically accumulated, outcome-linked evidence base for psychosocial intervention efficacy in living donor evaluation. Your clinical decisions generate data that improves national protocols. The work you do today will be visible in the outcomes achieved by every psychosocial professional who follows you in this system.
  • AI-administered CRI pre-assessment reduces social desirability bias documented in face-to-face screening contexts. Donors disclose more honestly to a non-judgmental adaptive interface. You receive richer, more honest psychosocial data before the formal encounter — improving your assessment confidence and decision accuracy.
  • LDCPR sub-analysis identifies the leading psychosocial denial factors: poor adherence (62%), psychiatric diagnosis (50%), cognitive concerns (33%), poor social support (25%). All four benefit from early identification. These are support needs the current system treats as exit conditions. This framework recategorizes them as early-intervention triggers — and your expertise is the intervention. Your clinical role expands significantly; it does not diminish.

Closing Moral Statement
The Moral Imperative — and the Question of Leadership
  • The Cold Ischemia Foundation’s position is unambiguous: the technology to transform this system exists today. The methodology is validated. The economic case is established. The equity argument is legally and clinically airtight. The OPTN data confirms where the system is failing and exactly which populations bear that failure. What remains is institutional will.
  • Every day the current system operates unchanged, medically and psychosocially appropriate donors exit for administrative reasons that are entirely preventable. The LDCPR confirms 15.2% of candidates walk away before reaching a decision. These are not people who stopped wanting to donate. These are people the system chose not to support — because building the support infrastructure requires a decision that has not yet been made.
  • OPTN state data shows exactly where the failure is worst: Louisiana, Mississippi, Alabama — the states with the highest disease burden, the weakest legislative protection, and the lowest donation rates. These populations were not failed by randomness. They were failed by a system never designed to serve them. This framework was designed specifically with those states as the primary intervention target.
  • This framework is not a technology project. It is a moral recalibration of what the transplant system owes to every person willing to offer the gift of life. The current system takes that willingness and returns a bureaucratic obstacle course. We owe these donors better. We have the tools to deliver better. The only remaining variable is institutional will — and that variable belongs to every leader in this room.
  • Project Donor-Flow is not the future of transplantation. It is the present — deployed at the centers willing to lead it. The question is not whether this standard will eventually be adopted nationally. It will. The question is whether your name is on the evidence base that shapes what it becomes. Leadership in transplant medicine has never been more consequential, or more directly available to those willing to claim it now.

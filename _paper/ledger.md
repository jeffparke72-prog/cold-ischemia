## A. Scale and state of the list
- SRTR24K (A) OPTN/SRTR 2024 Annual Data Report: Kidney. AJT 2026. 143,982 adult candidates listed for kidney transplant in 2024 ("approaching the prepandemic peak of 2019"); 49,249 adult additions in 2024 (record); 50,481 total additions; removals for death 3,743 in 2024 vs 4,734 in 2022; removals "too sick" rose to 4,901. Headline: progress offset by rising nonuse, longer cold ischemia, higher delayed graft function under broader sharing.
- SRTR24O (A) Overview 2024: 147,091 candidates on total kidney waiting list in 2024 (includes multiple listings); 29.3% of recovered kidneys not transplanted in 2024, up from 18.2% in 2013; procurement-biopsy kidneys 40.8% nonuse vs 6.4% non-biopsied; HCV-positive (Ab+/NAT-) kidney nonuse fell to 32.2% in 2024 from 49.5% in 2016.
- SRTR23 (A, older context) 2023 ADR: peak 146,637 in 2019; 142,962 in 2022; 47,838 additions in 2023 (+5.6%); 28,142 kidney transplants in 2023 (+7.0%, highest ever); nonuse 27.9% in 2023 from 26.6% in 2022; DCD 26.1% of deceased-donor kidney transplants.
- OPTN-REM (A, OPTN table via AOPO PDF) Kidney candidates removed — died / too sick: 2020 4,891/3,814; 2021 5,032/3,920; 2022 4,448/4,396; 2023 3,803/4,672; 2024 (Jan–Aug only) 2,187/3,132. NOTE: counts differ from SRTR ADR (population/definition).
- Transplantology2024 † (C) SRTR 2010–2021 analysis: discard rate 25% in 2021; "no recipient located" reached 60% of discard reasons.
- SURV (A, dated cohort 2007; USRDS 2014 chapter) 5-yr adjusted survival: dialysis 40.4% (HD 39.8, PD 49.2); deceased-donor transplant 73.7%; living-donor 87.0%. Selection caveat applies.
- WOLFE1999 (A, foundational) NEJM 341:1725–30. 228,552 dialysis pts; 46,164 waitlisted; 23,275 first cadaveric transplants 1991–97. Death rate 16.1/100 pt-yrs all dialysis; 6.3 waitlisted; 3.8 transplanted; long-term mortality 48–82% lower in recipients vs waitlisted; excess early risk; equal survival 5–673 days.

## C. Payment and incentive structure
- IOTA (A/B) CMS fact sheet: began 1 Jul 2025, ends 30 Jun 2031; PY1 103 kidney transplant hospitals in ~half of DSAs (other half comparison); 95 hospitals in PY2; one-sided risk PY1, two-sided PY2; score out of 100: achievement 60 (volume), efficiency 20 (organ offer acceptance), quality 20 (graft survival); max upside $15,000 per Medicare kidney transplant, max downside $2,000. 2026 final rule raised low-volume threshold from 11 to 15 transplants (B). NO PY1 results published as of Oct 2026.
- ETC (B) CMS ESRD Treatment Choices Model: 31% of HRRs, Jan 2021–Jun 2027; first-year report: 663 patients added to waitlist (+4%); through 3 years "no impact on transplant waitlisting or on living donor transplant rates."
- GANDER2019 (A) Gander JC et al. JAMA 2019;322(10):957–973. 1,478,564 patients, 6,511 facilities, USRDS 2000–2016; for-profit facilities → significantly lower waitlisting vs nonprofit; senior author (Patzer) attributes to incentives (higher margins with more dialysis patients). DO NOT quote the 29.8% secondary figure.
- MEDPAC26 (A) MedPAC July 2026 Data Book Sec 11: 2024 FFS Medicare spending for dialysis, dialysis drugs and ESRD labs $7.6 billion; FFS ESRD expenditures down 6% nominal 2023→2024 largely because of MA enrollment since 2021; ESRD patients +1%/yr 2019–2023; ~44% over 65 in 2023.
- VILLANI2024 † (A) Heliyon 2024: CMS-2728 forms 2007–2019, N = 133,414; 15% not informed about transplant; informed pts reached waitlist sooner; chain facilities more likely to inform but no higher waitlisting; independent facilities acquired by chains more likely to inform but less likely to waitlist after acquisition.
- SEGEV2018 (B) 388 dialysis pts: clinician-reported discussions of transplant did not increase waitlisting likelihood.

## D. Access (waitlisting, referral, poverty)
- KIDMED26 † (A) Kidney Medicine, Feb 2026: national cohort of maintenance dialysis patients; >10% had ≥1 social risk factor; associated with higher odds of not being transplanted or actively waitlisted; Black and American Indian patients higher odds of not listed/transplanted than White.
- PREDIAL26 † (A) "Pre-dialysis care and access type are associated with transplant referral, evaluation, and waitlisting" (2026): >half of adults started dialysis without AV access or pre-dialysis nephrology care; unprepared patients more often at for-profit and smaller facilities; more often male, younger, Black or Hispanic, uninsured, not informed of transplant, in high-poverty neighborhoods.
- NEIGHBOR † (A) "Residential neighborhood disadvantage and access to kidney transplantation": Black candidates in high-disadvantage neighborhoods less likely to receive transplant (AHR 0.60; 95% CI 0.58–0.62) vs White candidates in low-disadvantage neighborhoods. Journal unconfirmed.
- NONMED † (A) "Non-medical barriers in access to kidney transplantation: Perspectives from dialysis and transplant center staff across four US regions" (2026).
- PATZER09 (B, older) JASN 2009 neighborhood poverty/waitlisting.

## E. Offers, nonuse, ischemia
- CENTERAGG † (A) "Quantifying center-level aggressiveness in transplanting suboptimal, deceased donor kidneys in the United States" (PMC12795041): 193 adult centers accepted median 12.5% of offered non-suboptimal donors and 7.2% of suboptimal; widest variation for CIT >36 h (mOR 4.86); narrowest for increased-risk donors (mOR 2.06). Data 2021–2023.
- OFFERVAR (B) Earlier: kidneys offered median 7 times before acceptance; 2017 randomized study of 68 physicians — within-center variation 10× that among centers; 2023 survey Fleiss kappa 0.13; >7,500 recovered kidneys annually unused (statement in an abstract).
- DEBOUT2015 † (A) Debout A, et al. Kidney Int 2015;87(2):343–349 (DIVAT: 3,839 recipients 2000–2011). HR 1.013 graft failure per added hour; 30 h vs 6 h ≈ 40% higher risk; mortality HR 1.018/h. DCD-donor harm appears earlier (~19 h) in another analysis.
- FREIBURG26 † (A) Transplant Int, June 2026 (doi 10.3389/ti.2026.15840): 74 kidney pairs from older donors; CIT >12 h vs <8 h: DGF adjusted OR 6.30 (95% CI 1.52–26.06; p=0.011); death-censored graft survival unaffected; CIT >12 h tripled mortality risk; small single-centre.
- SHARING (B) Propensity analysis: regional sharing added ~2.9 h CIT vs local; OPTN committee: broader sharing increases allocation time, CIT and discard; network analysis of 106,160 kidneys: inter-OPO sharing lowers discard odds for high-volume OPOs, raises for low-volume; same-hospital CIT medians 3.3–29 h across 206 centers; weekend procurement more likely discarded than transplanted.

## F. Preservation and innovation
- MOERS2009 (A, foundational) Moers C et al. NEJM 2009;360:7–19; 672 recipients, paired kidneys; HMP vs cold storage; lower DGF, better 1-yr graft survival.
- MOERS10Y (C) ORS press release 5 Nov 2025 (BusinessWire): 10-year follow-up in NEJM; 27% lower risk graft failure; 99% follow-up response; complete 10-yr data 92%; DOI 10.1056/NEJMc2406608 ("c" suffix suggests correspondence — verify article type). Post-hoc per one ORS summary. Device-maker source.
- NMPSCS (A) Hosgood SA et al. Nature Med 2023;29:1511–1519 † NMP vs SCS in DCD kidneys: DGF 60.7% vs 58.5% (aOR 1.13) — feasible, safe, no DGF reduction.
- SLAGTER26 † (A) AJT (online late 2025 / 2026): "Normothermic versus hypothermic machine perfusion in kidney transplantation: a randomized controlled trial" — HMP+2h NMP vs HMP only; immediate graft function 39% (16/41) vs 49% (19/39); OR 0.68 (0.28–1.64; p=0.38); total CIT 9.5 vs 9.4 h; n=80.
- HOPE-ECD (A) RCT (expanded-criteria donors, end-HMPO2 after SCS) no improvement in graft survival/function; underpowered. US single-centre abstract: DGF 35.7% vs 49.2% (p=0.19) despite longer cold time.
- NATREVNEPH25 † (A) Nature Reviews Nephrology 2025 (doi 10.1038/s41581-025-00993-8): oxygen during HMP mixed results; clinical benefit of NMP unproven.
- FRTRA25 (A) "The Hitchhiker's guide to isolated organ perfusion: a journey to 2040" Frontiers in Transplantation 2025 †: renal HMP today reduces DGF and improves graft survival.
- HEMO2LIFE (B/C) OXYOP: 60 kidney grafts, 6 French centres, 2016, M101/HEMO2life additive, paired contralateral controls; investigators' 48-month patient survival 98.3% vs 86% (p=0.016); manufacturer: DGF 7% vs 26% in some subgroups; ClinicalTrials.gov NCT05050513 (observational follow-up, est. 116); OxyOp2 randomized trial NCT04181710 — no results found; porcine autotransplant lower fibrosis at 3 months. TREAT AS PRELIMINARY.
- EXPAND (A event) United Therapeutics UKidney (10 gene edits: six human genes added, four pig genes knocked out); NCT06878560; first transplant at NYU Langone announced 3 Nov 2025; 24-week monitoring, lifelong follow-up; no patient outcome data found for 2026. Earlier: MGH recipient's pig kidney graft survived 271 days (review), removed Oct 2025; NYU Nov 2024 graft removed Apr 2025.
- BHOC-SITE CIF's own evidence page: states BHOC has not been shown to prevent rejection or change transplant outcomes; "results and time gained are still to be proven"; "no oxygen carriers with full FDA approval for IV use in humans" (site's statement).

## G. Living donation
- LD2025 (C) PhillyVoice (2026): 6,522 living kidney donors in 2025; 6,419 in 2024; 6,290 in 2023; 6,867 in 2019; 7,237 living organ donors 2025 (+3%); 7,397 in 2019. Single source; verify against OPTN. HRSA interim: 5,923 living-donor transplants Jan–Oct 2025 (all organs, OPTN as of 24 Nov 2025).
- HOLD (A) Honor Our Living Donors Act enacted 3 Feb 2026 (Consolidated Appropriations Act, 2026, P.L. 119-75): prohibits considering the recipient's household income in donor reimbursement eligibility; donor-side test remains (priority at ≤350% of poverty guidelines in HRSA proposal); reimbursement up to $6,000 per organ for travel, lost wages, child and elder care; 21% of donors hit the $6,000 cap (physician group letter); NLDAC must report annually to Congress; HRSA notice 1 Jul 2026 requested comment; LODRP operated by Mayo Clinic Arizona and NLDAC. Federal Register public inspection docs 2026-13250 and 2026-19911.
- TUSHLA2015 † (A, older) CJASN 2015 consensus conference: financial impact on donors as potential source of decline; recommended standardized reimbursement and legal protections.
- AKF26 (B) AKF May 2026 report card: gains in several states, little to no progress in many; 28 states with LDPA-adjacent anti-discrimination laws (B).
- DOL (B) Department of Labor opinion letter (4 Sept; year unconfirmed): organ donation covered by FMLA.
- EKDA (B/C) H.R. 2687 (119th Cong.), introduced April 2025; Ways & Means and Energy & Commerce; refundable credit for non-directed living kidney donors: $10,000 for donation year and each of following four years ($50,000); kidneys removed after 31 Dec 2026; sunsets 31 Dec 2036; ~60 House cosponsors per campaign (Mar 2026); policy tracker: low chance of passage; Hawaii HR22/HCR22 2026 resolution urging passage. Still in committee as of spring 2026.
- AUSTRALIA (B) 2026 MJA: 253 living-donor kidney transplants in 2024, down from 354 in 2008; lower socio-economic groups disadvantaged.

## H. Medication, Medicaid and the poor patient
- PARTBID (B) Medicare Part B-ID: since Dec 2022 covers FDA-approved immunosuppressants indefinitely (earlier loss of Medicare 36 months after transplant for those not disabled/under 65); $97.10/month in 2023. ASPE/CEA analyses: extending coverage cost-saving; testimony cited CBO/OACT estimate up to $300M savings over ten years; 2012 NEJM report ~70% of programs reported a death or graft loss from inability to pay (via testimony — secondary).
- OBBBA (B) CBO via NKF/ASN: >11 million would lose Medicaid coverage, >$700B funding cut (NKF cites); work requirements 80 hrs/month from Jan 2027; provider tax limits; redeterminations every six months from Dec 2026 (fact brief, Gigafact 25 Aug 2026); nearly half of dialysis patients rely on Medicaid (NKF); transplant can exceed $400,000 + $10,000–14,000/yr immunosuppressants (ASN). Advocacy sources; enacted text should be verified.

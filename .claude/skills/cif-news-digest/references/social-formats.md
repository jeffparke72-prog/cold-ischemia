# Social packaging

Every item gets an X post, a Facebook post and a story card. The lead item of each issue (and any item the user asks for) also gets the X thread. Facts must match the issue exactly; if a fact is flagged VERIFY BEFORE POSTING, the post is too.

## X (Twitter) single post
- 270 characters or fewer, not counting the link (X shortens links to a fixed length).
- Structure: the number or the surprise, one plain sentence of meaning, the source tag, the link.
- At most two hashtags. No emoji walls. No "BREAKING".
- Example shape: "{Number} {what it measures}. {What that means for a family in one line.} ({Journal}, {year}) {link}"

## X thread (4 to 6 posts, lead item)
1. The hook: the single strongest verified number.
2. What the study actually did (design and sample, in plain words).
3. What it found, with the exact figure.
4. Who paid for it and what the authors disclosed.
5. What it does not show.
6. What a patient or care partner can ask their team, plus the source link.

## Facebook post (80 to 150 words)
Plain paragraphs, founder voice, no bullet lists. Open with the human stakes, give the finding, name the limit, end with one question the reader can take to their care team. Link last. End with "Educational, not medical advice."

## Story card (Facebook or Instagram Story, 1080 x 1920)
Text only, large type:
- Headline: 12 words or fewer.
- One number, huge.
- One line of context, 15 words or fewer.
- Source tag at the bottom: "{Journal}, {year}".
- Keep text out of the top 250 px and bottom 340 px (platform interface covers them).
Add alt text describing the card.

## Always
- Same accuracy standard as the issue. If a number could be misread, add the denominator.
- Never imply a study proves cause when it shows association.
- No donation asks. No sales language in digest posts.

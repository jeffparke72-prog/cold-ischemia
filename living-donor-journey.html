Where Willing Donors Are Lost

Half of evaluated candidates never get to donate.

The Living Donor Collective followed 2,107 consecutive kidney donor candidates at 10 U.S. programs. Here is what happened to them.

2,107candidates started an evaluation
1,578received a decision
790were approved to donate

Kasiske et al., Transplantation Direct, 2021 [3]

Why the others were not approved

Medical issues (other than kidney-disease risk)25.3%
Risk of future kidney disease18.5%
The candidate withdrew15.2%
A reason on the recipient’s side13.6%
Anatomy that posed a risk to the recipient10.3%
Psychosocial (not economic)10.3%
Other reasons6.4%

Share of 617 candidates not approved. Amber bars are reasons that better information, planning, or coordination can often change.

Many of these losses are preventable.

People withdraw when the process is slow, confusing, or costly. They are declined when a fixable issue, such as unmeasured blood pressure, weight, smoking, or a missing support plan, surfaces late. Recipients change status, and the donor’s whole evaluation is lost instead of redirected.

This tool exists to move those discoveries to the beginning: to help you understand the process, spot what you can work on now, and arrive at a transplant center prepared.

A Bigger, Smoother System

Today, every transplant center starts from zero.

A donor who is declined at one program, whose recipient changes, or who moves, usually starts over somewhere else: new forms, repeated tests, a new wait. Project Donor-Flow proposes a connected system.

Siloed today

Each center runs its own intake and evaluation. Records rarely travel with the donor.

Connected: Project Donor-Flow

One shared intake, one portable record, one coordinated hand-off to the right program.

Faster decisionsFixable issues surface at first contact instead of months in.
Lower overheadTests and records are shared, not repeated at each program.
Better accessDonors are routed to programs and paired exchanges that fit them.
More donorsA clear, respectful process keeps willing people from walking away.

This is the Cold Ischemia Foundation’s proposal. Read the full framework on the Project Donor-Flow page.

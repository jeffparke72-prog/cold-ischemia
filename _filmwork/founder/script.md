# WHY WE EXIST: A MESSAGE FROM THE FOUNDER

## 1. HELLO

Hi. I'm Jeff, the founder of the Cold Ischemia Foundation.

If you're watching this, kidney disease has probably walked into your life, or into the life of someone you love. Maybe you're on dialysis. Maybe you're waiting for a call that hasn't come. Maybe you're the care partner, the one driving, scheduling, filling the pill box, and holding the whole family together.

I want to start with what nobody said to me: I see you.

I'm a patient. I've lived this system from the inside. I know the chair. I know the waiting. I know what it feels like to be handed a pamphlet when what you needed was an answer. And I know the moment you realize that nobody is coming to explain it, and you have to figure it out yourself.

That is why I started this foundation. And that is why I made this video.

## 2. WHO WE ARE (logo reveal)

So let me introduce us properly. This is the Cold Ischemia Foundation.

In medicine, cold ischemia is the time an organ can survive once it's been cut off from what keeps it alive. We took that name for a reason. We have watched the same thing happen to people. Cut off from answers. Cut off from respect. Cut off from any real say in their own care. Slowly going cold.

Our whole job is to end that cold interval.

Here is what that looks like. We build free tools you can use tonight: a living donor assessment, a state-by-state directory of transplant centers with direct phone numbers, guides written for care partners. We build the guides and the mentors who have been where you are. And we build the confidence to understand your own body, your own numbers, and your own choices.

Because you have choices. If you remember one sentence from this video, remember that one.

## 3. WHY THIS VIDEO

So why this video, and why now?

Because every week, someone newly diagnosed types "kidney disease" into a search bar and lands on a page that feels caring and independent. And too often, that page is paid for by the companies that profit from what happens next.

I'm not calling anyone a villain. I'm telling you that you deserve to know who is paying for the voice that is talking to you. Nobody else is going to say it. So I will.

## 4. THE RECEIPTS

Start with the size of it. Thirty-seven million Americans have kidney disease, and the CDC says eighty-seven percent of the adults who have it do not know. Most people find out when it is already a crisis.

And when it is a crisis, there are two companies. DaVita and Fresenius treat roughly three in four dialysis patients in this country. DaVita alone brought in thirteen point six billion dollars last year. In 2024 it paid more than thirty-four million to settle federal kickback allegations, without admitting liability. Those are allegations. The revenue is not.

Meanwhile only about one patient in seven dialyzes at home. And a 2025 analysis of seventy-two studies found that seeing a kidney specialist early cut catheter starts by more than half. Early. Before the crisis. That is what prevention looks like, and it does not bill by the treatment.

## 5. WHO PAYS FOR THE VOICE

Now here is the part some people won't like.

In 2017, researchers in the New England Journal of Medicine looked at the hundred and four largest patient advocacy organizations in America. Eighty-three percent took money from drug, device, or biotech companies. Only one of the hundred and four said plainly that it did not.

Kidney care is no different. The American Kidney Fund has been reported to draw roughly eighty percent of its donor base from DaVita and Fresenius. It denies steering patients, and the Justice Department declined to join the whistleblower suit. The National Kidney Foundation's corporate membership lists Boehringer Ingelheim, Novo Nordisk, and Vertex. The American Association of Kidney Patients lists DaVita, Fresenius, and Amgen. And Dialysis Patient Citizens was founded with an educational grant from DaVita.

None of that is illegal. Taking industry money is legal, and common, and some of these groups do real work. I'm not telling you what to think about any of them. I'm telling you to look at the table.

## 6. HOW WE ARE DIFFERENT (comparison table)

Now look at us. No donations. No funding, gifts, grants, or sponsorships from drugmakers, device makers, dialysis companies, insurers, hospital systems, or any advocacy group they fund. We charge fees only for services you ask for, at posted rates, and a fee never buys influence. No drugmaker, dialysis company, insurer, or hospital system has a seat on our board.

Ask any organization five questions. Who pays you? Who governs you? Who shapes your message? What can't you say? And who do you answer to? Our answers are simple. Patients and care partners. That is all. And because we have no sponsor to protect, we can name every institution.

## 7. WHAT INDEPENDENCE BUILDS

Independence is not a slogan. It is a structure, and structures build things.

Free toolkits with no paywall. A weekly investigation called the Inside Files. A public scorecard of how Congress acts on transplant and kidney care. Formal demand letters to the agencies that run this system.

And a spotlight on what is really happening. In 2024, three thousand seven hundred and forty-three people were removed from the kidney waiting list because they died. Twenty-nine percent of the kidneys recovered that year were never transplanted. Those are not numbers. Those are people. And we will keep saying so.

## 8. YOUR PART

So here is what I'm asking.

Don't take my word for it. Not mine, not theirs. Read their Form 990. Find their sponsor page. Read the board list. Ask what they have ever said against the people who fund them. We built a free Advocacy Assessment that walks you through it.

Then come with us. Use the tools. Send this to a care partner who is exhausted tonight. Volunteer. Tell us where the system failed you, because your experience is evidence.

We started this foundation because we were tired of waiting for somebody else to do it. We are not asking for permission. We are building it, in the open, with nobody to protect but you.

You have choices. And you are not alone.

Why this course exists

You were handed a diagnosis. Nobody handed you the system.

Patients and care partners are taught to count fluid, memorize lab ranges, and show up on time. They are not taught who profits from the chair, what federal rules govern the clinic, why a kidney sits on ice for hours and then gets thrown away, or how to file a grievance without losing their treatment slot. That omission is not an accident. A population that does not understand the system cannot challenge it.

The Unspoken Curriculum closes that gap. It covers the history, the economics, the regulations, the ethics, and the philosophy of kidney failure in the United States — the material that sits behind the brochure. It is built for people who have never been asked what they think, and who are more than capable of thinking.

This is not a test of memorized trivia. Every question places you inside a real situation — a discharge threat, an organ offer, a conversation about stopping treatment, a care partner who has disappeared into the role — and asks you to reason your way to the structurally sound answer. Each answer comes with an explanation and its source. You learn from every question, whether you get it right or not.

The course serves three purposes: to protect the person in the chair, to protect the person holding everything together at home, and to equip both to turn lived experience into documented, submitted, undeniable evidence. That is how a legacy outlasts a diagnosis.

Cold Ischemia Foundation · A game for Christmas dinner

The Long Table

One phone, passed hand to hand after the plates are cleared. Twenty-four cards for the conversations a family only manages once a year, when everyone is finally in the same room.

Between dinner and dessert

Why the table

Most of the year, a family living with illness talks in fragments. A text sent from the clinic parking lot. A call on the drive home. A sister three states away who hears about the hospital stay a week after it happened. Then December comes, the extra leaf goes into the table, and everyone sits in one room long enough to finish a sentence.

This game is for that hour. It isn't a quiz and nobody wins. Some cards ask the person whose health it is to tell you something, and ask everyone else to listen without fixing. Some put a written rule on the table, the kind clinics rarely volunteer. Some settle one practical thing before the coats come out. Some hand you a question to carry into January.

That happens to be most of what a true advocate does. They write things down. They know the rule. They ask the specific question. They make sure the person at the center is heard, not handled. Nobody pays them for it, and tonight, nobody needs to. It's just your family, and a deck of cards.

Before the first card

For whoever starts

Read this part alone, before you suggest the game. It takes a minute.

Five steps

How to run it

  1. Wait until the plates are cleared. Say it takes about twenty minutes and nobody has to play.
  2. Ask the person whose health it is, quietly and first, whether they're up for it tonight. If the answer is no, put the phone away. That answer is the whole point.
  3. Read the first card out loud. It is always the same card, and it is always for them.
  4. After each card, hand the phone to the next person. They read the next card.
  5. Stop while it still feels good, even halfway through the deck. Before dessert, choose three things for the Table Pledge.
The pass

Any card, any time

Anyone can pass any card, for any reason, and nobody asks why. Tap Pass and the card goes quietly back in the box.

A pass is not the game failing. It is the game working. A family that can hear "not tonight" without flinching is a family a patient can trust with the harder things.

If a card opens something heavy, you can close the phone and simply stay at the table together. That counts.

Whose health it is

Their story, their pace

They decide what gets shared, who hears it, and what stays at this table. Care partners get a voice too, but nobody speaks for someone who is sitting right there. No decisions about their care get made tonight without them.

One rule is not up for discussion: no one is asked to be a living donor at this table. Living donation is a free, private choice a person makes on their own, in their own time. Nobody owes it, and nobody asks for it over dinner.

Holidays can bring grief close. If anyone at the table, or you, is in crisis, the 988 Suicide & Crisis Lifeline answers by call or text at 988.

The deck

Four suits, twenty-four cards

Each suit practices one habit of a true advocate. The deck is shuffled, but the first card is always a Tell It card, because the night should start by listening.

Names put a place card at each setting and tell the phone whose turn is next. They stay on this device.
